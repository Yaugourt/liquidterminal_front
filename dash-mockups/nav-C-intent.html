Nav proposal C

Intent-first: left the verbs, right the nouns.

The left rail is organised by what you came to do: Trade, Track, Explore, Learn, Build. Not by how the protocol is built. A newcomer who has never heard of HIP-3 still knows which of five verbs matches their question. The right rail is the opposite axis: it follows the object you are currently looking at, and carries it across the verbs.

The filing rule, one sentence

A page goes under the verb of the question you arrive with, and if that question contains the word “my”, it goes to Track, whatever the data underneath is.

Core / EVM is a facet, never a section

HyperCore and HyperEVM are two halves of one chain, so they are a layer switch in the scope bar and a badge on every row, present on every page where the answer differs, absent where it does not. Objects that straddle both keep one page and show both sides.

Left rail · the verbs

Five intents, never nested twice. A group label carries a one-line caption so the verb is unambiguous on first visit. Collapsed, the group separators keep the five blocks readable at 56px.

Scope bar · the perimeter

The MarketScopeBar pattern, generalised to every section: siblings on the left, the Core / EVM / Both layer switch on the right. It is the only place the two halves of Hyperliquid are named.

Right rail · the nouns

Watching (yours, everywhere) · On this page (anchors) · Other intents (the same object seen from the four other verbs). It is what pays back the cost of splitting an object across sections.
